# Pricing and Launch Plan — Liquidity Sweep Detector MVP

## Purpose

This document explains how the Liquidity Sweep Detector MVP can be packaged, priced, and launched after the first working version exists.
It is written for future Kaino sessions so product execution does not stop at engineering.

It covers:
- launch posture
- pricing models
- early distribution strategy
- beta plan
- first-customer plan
- simple revenue math
- positioning guidance

---

## 1. Launch philosophy

The first launch should not be framed as a mass-market polished trading platform.
It should be framed as a focused, explainable beta or early-access tool for traders who already understand liquidity concepts.

That means the product message should emphasize:
- structure
- clarity
- time-saving chart analysis
- probability-based detection
- configurable logic

It should not emphasize:
- guaranteed profits
- sure-shot signals
- financial advice
- automated winning entries

---

## 2. Recommended go-to-market sequence

## Phase 1 — Private validation
Target:
- 10 to 25 traders
- trusted contacts, communities, or niche trading users

Goal:
- validate that the signal output makes sense
- identify false positives
- understand which settings matter most
- collect screenshots and testimonials if genuine

## Phase 2 — Controlled beta launch
Target:
- 25 to 100 users

Goal:
- refine positioning
- test onboarding clarity
- test pricing interest
- identify support burden

## Phase 3 — Paid launch
Target:
- early retail users
- trading educators
- prop-style traders

Goal:
- validate revenue model
- refine retention strategy
- learn which segment converts best

## 3. Positioning options

## Option A — Indicator-style tool
Message:
> Detect likely liquidity sweeps and reclaim behavior faster.

Good for:
- experienced traders
- TradingView-like audience

## Option B — Market structure assistant
Message:
> A chart intelligence tool for traders who want clearer sweep and reversal context.

Good for:
- more serious users
- less signal-buyer framing

## Option C — Smart money workflow tool
Message:
> A visual sweep-confirmation assistant for SMC-style traders.

Good for:
- niche audience already using SMC language
- narrower but higher-intent positioning

### Recommended MVP positioning
Use a hybrid of B and C:
> Detect likely liquidity sweeps using prior highs/lows, reclaim behavior, and confirmation logic.

## 4. Pricing models

## Model 1 — One-time purchase
Examples:
- ₹1,999
- ₹2,999
- ₹4,999

### Pros
- simple to understand
- fast early revenue
- lower friction than recurring plans for new users

### Cons
- weaker recurring revenue
- users may expect lifetime updates
- harder long-term business model unless new versions or upsells exist

### Best use
- early access launch
- first 50 to 200 users

## Model 2 — Monthly subscription
Examples:
- ₹499/month
- ₹999/month
- ₹1,499/month

### Pros
- recurring revenue
- better business stability
- easier to fund continuous improvements

### Cons
- higher trust needed
- support and retention matter much more
- users compare it against other trading tools monthly

### Best use
- after the product is clearly useful
- after initial trust and onboarding improve

## Model 3 — Hybrid model
Example:
- one-time early access fee
- optional pro monthly tier later

### Recommended hybrid path
- early beta / launch users pay one-time lower price
- later users move to subscription or premium plan

This is often the safest path for MVP.

## 5. Suggested pricing path for this product

## Stage 1 — Beta
- free or invite-only
or
- small commitment price like ₹999 to filter serious users

## Stage 2 — Early access
- ₹1,999 to ₹2,999 one-time

## Stage 3 — Paid recurring
- ₹999/month for active dashboard/tool access
- optional annual discount later

### Why this path works
- trust is built before higher pricing
- early users help validate utility
- you avoid overpricing before the detector is mature

## 6. Simple revenue math examples

## One-time pricing example
If 1000 users buy at ₹1,999:
- gross revenue ≈ ₹19,99,000

If 1000 users buy at ₹2,999:
- gross revenue ≈ ₹29,99,000

If 1000 users buy at ₹4,999:
- gross revenue ≈ ₹49,99,000

These are gross figures, not pure profit.

## Subscription pricing example
If 1000 users subscribe at ₹999/month:
- monthly revenue ≈ ₹9,99,000

If 1000 users subscribe at ₹1,499/month:
- monthly revenue ≈ ₹14,99,000

Again, this depends on retention and acquisition cost.

## 7. Realistic cost buckets

Even a self-built product has operating costs.
Expect:
- domain and hosting
- app infrastructure
- payment fees
- customer support time
- analytics or email tools
- optional market data cost
- content/marketing spend

### Lean self-built posture
Early MVP cash cost may remain relatively low if:
- engineering is self-done
- marketing begins organically
- market coverage is narrow

But growth costs rise once paid acquisition begins.

## 8. Recommended customer segments in order

### 1. Retail traders
Best first segment because:
- easiest access
- fastest feedback
- simplest sales motion

### 2. Trading educators / communities
Good second segment because:
- they can influence adoption
- they may want group access or white-labeled value later

### 3. Prop-style disciplined traders
Useful niche if the detector proves selective and trustworthy

### 4. Brokers
Broker sales should come much later.
This is not the first sales motion.

## 10. Beta feedback framework

During beta, ask users:
- did the signal make sense?
- was the chart readable?
- were there too many low-quality signals?
- which settings were confusing?
- would you pay for this?
- what feature matters next: alerts, watchlist, journaling, or better confirmations?

Use structured feedback instead of vague chat opinions when possible.

---

## 11. Launch risks

### Risk: users expect perfect accuracy
Mitigation:
- use careful wording
- show explanation behind signals
- avoid hype claims

### Risk: noisy signals damage trust
Mitigation:
- stricter defaults
- narrow market/timeframe support initially
- hide failed/noisy states by default if needed

### Risk: product is seen as “just another indicator”
Mitigation:
- emphasize explanation and workflow
- show confidence states and reclaim logic
- make UI cleaner than typical cluttered tools

### Risk: support load rises too early
Mitigation:
- keep beta cohort small
- document default settings and scope clearly

## 13. Recommendation for first monetization move

The safest early move is:
1. private beta
2. invite-based early access
3. low-friction one-time early access fee
4. subscription only after value is obvious

This reduces pressure while the logic is still being refined.

---

## 14. Final recommendation

For MVP, do not optimize first for maximum price.
Optimize first for:
- trust
- usability
- clarity
- signal quality
- real feedback from actual traders

If the product genuinely saves time and improves interpretation, pricing can become stronger later.
